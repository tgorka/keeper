### 99.1 — A gate agent

- **`agents-99-room`: #502, open, final `9404236c`** (amended from `0ddb8359`; report-99room-fix1.md).
  - **What a person gets:** an operator declares a gate agent with one `[[gate]]` table per outside system. `keeper-agentd agents gate <agent> <system>` makes that system's room and session once. The system's account can post in the room but can take nothing: it sits at power 0, and its claim, power levels, invite, kick and redaction are all refused 403.
  - **Proved:**
    - #1: `home::tests::gate_table_grammar`, plus the zone half.
    - #5: live `agents_gate_is_idempotent`.
    - #8: live `gate_room_power_levels_hold`, in an encrypted and a clear room.
  - **Built differently from the plan:**
    - The peer sits at 0, not at Q5's 50; Q3 supersedes Q5.
    - Live tests run on `KEEPER_AGENTS_SMOKE_*`, not `KEEPER_TEST_SYNAPSE`.
  - **Owed:** `agents gate` run on electra.
- **`agents-99-sync`: #503, open, final `97da6c5b`** (from `3f0fc0d1`).
  - **What a person gets:** the outside system asks and is answered within its deadline. If the deadline passes first, it gets a ticket at the deadline, timed by the homeserver's clock, and the late answer arrives as the ticket's result. Requests and tickets are bounded per hour, and open tickets are also capped by `[limits].max_concurrent_sessions` across the agent's gates.
  - **Proved:**
    - #2: `gate::tests::gate_request_from_event`, plus the classify gate arm.
    - #3: live. The ticket came 3 048 ms after the request, inside the 2 700–3 300 ms window.
    - #6: the concurrency bound.
    - #7: checked inside the live sync test. `keeper-agentd` is non-dumpable, so the planned per-pid `/proc` check could not run; the egress idiom replaced it, and live mutant L01 (a leaked listener) was killed.
    - #9: `gate::tests::gate_tickets_are_rate_limited_per_peer`.
  - **Owed:** a sync request answered on electra.
- **`agents-99-tickets`: #504, open, final `797a5f85`** (from `6c113af1`).
  - **What a person gets:** an outside system's request reaches another agent only after one of the drive's pinned people approves it. Approvals are asked on one card per peer per hour, in the gate room. The host then delegates to an agent the gate lists and posts that agent's reply as the ticket's result.
  - **Proved:**
    - #4: fake ports `parks::gate_delegate_needs_a_person` and two siblings; live `gate_async_ticket_session_callback`.
    - #6 (tokens): `brief.limits.tokens == 4000`.
    - #10: live `gate_cards_coalesce_per_peer_per_window`. Five tickets made one request event and four edits, and two decisions consumed exactly two records.
  - **Measured live:** a delegate's reply carries its home drive's label, so the room's sink blocks the callback. "Exactly one `result`" therefore holds live only through rung 4's release. This rung's live test now ends `unreleased`, because its host runs no proxy (report-99tickets, "MEASURED").
  - **Owed:**
    - on electra, an async ticket approved from tgorka's device and delegated;
    - the card drawn and decided on kalypso and hesperia.
- **`agents-99-ui`: #506, open, final `c966a4de`** (from `9837f8b9`; the stack's top).
  - **What a person gets:** in keeper, a gate room shows what it is.
    - Requests are quoted as data under the gate's word: answered, ticket, result or refused.
    - The header names the system and its account.
    - There is nothing to write with: no composer, reply, reaction or file drop.
    - The hour's card is decided line by line, or with *Deny all*.
  - **Proved:**
    - pure and SDK tests: `gate_room::tests::*` and `timeline::brief_tests::a_gate_room_draws_a_request_by_the_gates_word_on_it`;
    - vitest: 6 717 passed after the fix round;
    - the live scope assertion;
    - a browser run over the mock shell at 1 280 and 420 px. The card measured 560 and 388 px, with no overflow.
  - **Not built:** the ticket is shown by its id, not linked to its session (DW-1370).
  - **Owed:**
    - the real room and card on hesperia, kalypso and the Android tablet;
    - the first ticket's notification offering no *Approve once*.
- **Operator-verified items** (epic § 99.1, *Operator-verified*), both owed:
  - the electra run, end to end;
  - a webhook bridge appservice on tuwunel, if a webhook system is wanted (DW-415).

## What the independent reviews caught

Every review below was Claude Fable 5.1 (the bmad-review lane) reading Opus 5.5 code, so none is fully model-independent. Each rung had one review round, and no fix round was reviewed again.

- **Rung 1: review-99room-1.md — CHANGES REQUIRED.**
  - **MEDIUM R99R1-01 → fixed in place (R366 amended, R367).** A room found by its name or status was adopted without checking who made it, its power levels or its encryption, so a room someone else made could become the gate's room, and there what #8 proves (the peer at 0, the gate alone at 100) would not hold. The review judged that the peer alone could not stage such a room then, but a zone agent under injection could, and so could a later rung's child room (the review marks that path `[INFERENCE]`). The fix is `gate::adoption`: this copy alone made the room, its levels match row by row, and its encryption matches the file. Otherwise `agents gate` refuses with exit 2 and names the room. One live case is a room the peer made under the gate room's name.
  - **LOWs 02–06 → all fixed in place (R367).** A room no person is in, when no approver has a pinned key; the `always_on` refusal untested; refusals untested through the verb; wording pins; the session parser not requiring `untrusted`. The review's DW candidates became DW-1332…DW-1336.
- **Rung 2: review-99sync-1.md — CHANGES REQUIRED.**
  - **HIGH R99S-01 → fixed in place (R369 amended, R370 FIX 01).** A gate's dispatch did not mirror its offer: `ask_human`, `journal_append` and any MCP tool ran if the model named them. The lane had deleted the guard that surviving mutant M25 pointed at. The fix: `AllowedTools::named` refuses any name the turn was not offered, the guard is restored, `ask::offered` and `memory::offered_in` are false in a gate, and a gate turn gets no MCP tools.
  - **MEDIUM R99S-02 → fixed in place (R368 amended, R370 FIX 02).** The peer's own `origin_server_ts` set the request allowance and the deadline, so a federated peer could void the hourly bound. It is now clamped to the server's current time.
  - **LOWs 03–08 → fixed in place (R370 FIX 03–08).**
    - a takeover re-took answered requests;
    - every refusal was echoed;
    - concurrency was counted per session, not per agent;
    - an async ticket was logged as said when it was not;
    - control characters were allowed in request names;
    - wording pins.

    The residues are DW-1343, DW-1344, DW-1345 and DW-1346.
- **Rung 3: review-99tickets-1.md — ACCEPTED.** No HIGH or MEDIUM.
  - **LOWs 01–04 → fixed in place (R373):**
    - a decision is judged against the label its ticket was asked under, at the seat and at the room's front;
    - the audit comes before the ticket is paid;
    - a card the room did not take is asked again;
    - every hand-off refusal is tested by its cause.
  - **LOWs 05 and 06 → recorded:** DW-1343 widened, DW-1341 extended.
  - **The docs nit → DW-1352.** DW-1353 and DW-1354 were also opened.
- **Rung 4: review-99untrusted-1.md — ACCEPTED.** The file names its reviewer only as "a different model from the author"; stack-notes for 2026-10-13 says Fable.
  - **LOW–MEDIUM R99U1-01 → fixed in place (R376 FIX 01).** A held request took a second callback and could be held again on a second declassify record. A second callback of a held request is now ignored (`RESULT_HELD`) and runs no turn, so the first card decides.
  - **LOWs 02–03 and info 04:**
    - 02: a journal card was not retried, and an entry made after a drive read was told it "waits". Both fixed: the card is retried, and the entry is refused at the call with `GATE_JOURNAL_NARROWED`. The owner-DM route is DW-1364.
    - 03: `refuse_held` could leave a request open. Fixed.
    - 04: the release's SHA check guards only against a corrupted record. Recorded in R374's text.

    DW-1365 records #6's accepted reading.
- **Rung 5: review-99ui-1.md — CHANGES REQUIRED.**
  - **MEDIUM R99UI1-01 → fixed in place (R379 FIX 01).** The peer could edit a request after the gate answered it, and keeper then drew the gate's word over text the gate never saw. An edited request is now drawn as an ordinary message.
  - **LOW/minor 02–08 → fixed in place (R377 and R378 amended, R379), except 08:**
    - `rebrief` did not re-read the gate view;
    - composer affordances stayed in the room;
    - *Deny all* said "nothing was decided" after a partial failure and offered already-sent rows again;
    - the deadline was never drawn;
    - prose pins;
    - the peer's display name was unquoted on request lines;
    - 08, a failed scope send never repaired, was recorded in DW-1371.

## What went wrong in the process

1. **A surviving mutant was read as dead code (rung 2).**
   - **Cause:** M25 removed a second gate-kind guard and survived. The lane deleted the guard as redundant and recorded that in R369 (report-99sync § Mutation). The survivor was in fact the signal: the test never called what only that guard refused. As a result, `ask_human`, `journal_append` and MCP tools could run in a gate, which is the epic's own failure-shape bullet 4 (review-99sync-1 R99S-01).
   - **Cost:** a HIGH finding, a fix round, R369 rewritten, and R370.
   - **What changed:** dispatch now mirrors the offer, the guard is back, and the test injects each tool in a world where it could otherwise run.
2. **The plan assumed a delegate's reply could reach the gate room.**
   - **Cause:** 99.1 #4 expects "exactly one `result`". Rung 3's live test measured that the reply carries the delegate's home drive label. That label never includes the peer, so the room's sink blocks the reply (report-99tickets, "MEASURED").
   - **Cost:** #4's result path is proved only by fake ports in its own rung. The live release needed a later rung (R372 → R374).
   - **What changed:** results like this are now held for the owner's declassification, and `docs/agents.md` § *Handing a request on* says so.
3. **Each rung was built on a parent still under review.**
   - **Cause:** every rung was rebased onto its parent's fix commit. Rung 1 was also moved from `3710a2c7` onto epic 98's final `13b88251`. Conflicts came up in the two ledgers, `gate.rs`, `docs/agents.md`, `agent_turns.rs` and `deciding.rs`, and were resolved by keeping both sides (each report's header).
   - **Ruling numbers:** rulings were numbered provisionally (R-NEW-1/2 in rungs 4–5) and renumbered later. Rung 1's fix lane warned that R367 might collide with rung 2's (report-99room-fix1 § Rulings).
   - **Cost:** one rebase per rung plus the renumbering. No collision was landed.
4. **delectra's disk filled on almost every rung.**

   | lane | disk |
   | --- | --- |
   | rung 1 fix | 98% (822 MB free) |
   | rung 2 | 98% → 76% |
   | rung 2 fix | 99% (493 MB free) → 6.6 GB free |
   | rung 3 | 99% → 92% |
   | rung 3 fix | 92% → 73% |
   | rung 4 | a mutation run filled it; 4.4 GB of incremental builds removed, 98% → 84% |

   - **Cost:** five lanes pruned by hand, and one mutation run was lost.
   - **What changed:** mutation scripts now run with `CARGO_INCREMENTAL=0`, and pruning happens under `delectra.lock`.
5. **hesperia was unreachable for the UI rung.**
   - **Cause:** `ssh mac` timed out in both 99ui lanes (report-99ui § Shell crate; report-99ui-fix1 § Could not do).
   - **Cost:** no macOS check ran, so the macOS build of the changed VMs rests on CI's macOS job. The shell crate itself was not touched, and the reports' greps found no shell caller of the changed types.
6. **Smaller tool faults.**
   - `delectra-gate.sh … HEAD` failed because the bundle's `HEAD` was not fetched. It was rerun with the branch name (report-99sync-fix1).
   - Mutant F05a did not compile and was replaced by F05a2. Mutant U16 hit pre-existing code; its re-aimed U16b was killed locally only.
   - After rung 3's rebase, a test overflowed the 2 MiB test-thread stack. It was fixed with `Box::pin`, not hidden behind `RUST_MIN_STACK` (report-99tickets § Gates).
7. **The same environmental failures appear in every report, and each lane classified them again by hand:**
   - inotify EMFILE in `a_served_sessions_second_turn_opens_no_file_under_log`;
   - the BusyBox `run_linux` tests, with `KEEPER_TEST_BUSYBOX` unset;
   - `parks::mcp::mcp_diagnostics_are_redacted`, under full-suite load;
   - `setup-code-scanner.test.tsx`, in symlinked worktrees;
   - font 403s in the browser runs.
8. **No model-independent review.**
   - **Cause:** GPT-6 Astra's quota ran out on 2026-10-10 (stack-notes). All five reviews were Fable 5.1 reading Opus 5.5 code, and the five fix rounds were pushed without a second review.
   - **Cost:** this epic's failure shape is the lethal trifecta through a side door, so this is its largest remaining review risk.

**Program-wide incidents:** all of these happened before epic 99's lanes started on 2026-10-12: the `reasoning_extraction` refusal (10-05), the coordinator's deaths and resumes (10-06…10-11), the OOM kills (10-10/10-11), and the Paseo daemon restart of 2026-10-11 06:16:55. The 99 briefs were written on 10-11 at 07:10, after that restart (stack-notes). No epic-99 artifact records any of them touching this epic.

The CDP token leaked in 98tablet's transcript on 2026-10-12. The 99ui lanes used the same desktop Chrome afterwards, and their reports say neither the token nor the CDP URL was printed. Rungs 3–5 rebased with rerere off (`-c rerere.enabled=false`).

## What to keep

- **Live tests against a real homeserver with two real users.**
  - They found the sink-blocked callback, which fake ports could not.
  - They measured the deadline by the server's timestamps (3 048 ms).
  - They proved a 403 on every state change the peer tried (claim, power levels, invite, kick, redaction), in both kinds of room, while its messages were accepted.
  - They killed live mutants: the peer at 50, a leaked listener, and an ignored adoption verdict (reports 99room, 99room-fix1, 99sync).
- **Honest survivors.**
  - F01d was reported as a backup check that no test can see.
  - Branches not proved by mutation were listed: the per-request label reset, `send_brief`'s label, and the live scope assertion.
  - T14's survivor became an assertion that kills it.

  M25 is the one counter-example; see item 1 above.
- **One fix round, each finding handled as fix → test → mutant.** Across the five rounds, 65 fix-round mutants were killed and one documented survivor remains.
- **The next rung told what changed.** Every report ends with `signatures_for_rung_N` and names the places where the next rung plugs in.
- **Typed refusals instead of wording.** `GateZoneCause` and `InviteRefusal` were added, and tests assert constants rather than sentences (the fixes for R99R1-05, R99S-08 and R99UI1-06).
- **Fail-closed by construction, confirmed by the reviewers.** Each review's "Checked and clean" section confirms three things:
  - nothing is sent before `check_sink` answers;
  - the peer's non-message events count for nothing;
  - a blocked result ends refused with a reason.
- **Secrets kept out.** Every live log was grep-counted for secrets, with 0 hits in every rung, and no browser run printed the CDP token or URL.
- **DW blocks reserved per rung** in `rung-plan-98-99.json`. Five rungs ran in parallel without a single DW collision.
- **Rebase with rerere off, and keep both sides of a ledger conflict.**
- **Measure the UI.** Card widths were measured at both widths, and the ARIA snapshots were left for the reviewer, who read them.
