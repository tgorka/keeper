## What was measured

1. **Every manual "stop this answer" ends the turn and lets the synthesiser keep talking.** `turn.rs:215`: `(Speaking, Silence) => (Idle, [ReleaseMicrophone])` — no `StopSpeaking`; `Silence` was designed as "the synthesiser finished on its own" (`:280-282`) and only `Effect::StopSpeaking` reaches `port.stop_speaking()` (`mod.rs:604`). Yet `voice_stop_speaking()` is `transition(Silence)` (`voice_ipc.rs:504-509`) and it is what the mic button while speaking (`bot-voice-mic.tsx:146-147`) and the tray's "Stop this answer" (`tray.rs:809` → `voice_reach.rs:106` → `:73`) call. The hotkey and `keeper://voice/talk` are `ReachAsk::Talk`, which in `Speaking` is a no-op (`voice_reach.rs:104`). Unchanged since epic 62 (`744624d`). On iOS `Worker::stop()` clears everything but the `speaking` flag (`voice_ios.rs:749-756`, `:838-839`), so after the button every transcript is still `SpeechDetected` and dropped by an `Idle` turn — the phone is deaf to "stop" and "nixie" until the utterance ends by itself.
2. **On the Mac the word cannot be heard, by construction — on a premise the tree already contradicts.** `platform.rs:99` `MACOS.full_duplex: false`; `mod.rs:494-503` releases the microphone before `Speak`; the port never emits `SpeechDetected` (`voice_macos.rs:64-76`). The premise ("macOS has no AVAudioSession, so nothing keeps keeper's voice out of the transcript", `platform.rs:16-19`) is contradicted by Story 22.7: `kAudioUnitSubType_VoiceProcessingIO` on macOS subtracts the output device's mix — measured at ~−24 dB on hesperia (`epics.md:3400-3403`) — and `AVAudioEngine.inputNode.setVoiceProcessingEnabled(true)` is the same call the iOS port makes (`voice_ios.rs:1543`), already linked on the Mac.
3. **The 30 s is Hermes' first token; keeper's own share is two seconds — and on long answers, everything after the first sentence.** hesperia's `keeper.db`, the turn the owner reported (2026-09-05 17:17:38): `ttft_ms = 28 550`, `duration_ms = 30 524`, `prompt_tokens = 20 870` for a conversation of 6 messages / 989 bytes. keeper sends a kilobyte; Hermes prefills twenty thousand tokens of its own context before the first token, every turn (the last ten Hermes turns: TTFT 11–73 s, prompt 20k–91k). Nothing polls; the chat is one SSE stream (`chat.rs:989-1056`). keeper's waits: the 1.8 s end-of-utterance pause (`turn.rs:53`), one connect per turn (a new `reqwest::Client` per turn, `http.rs:81-104`), and **speech only after the whole answer** (`close` → `answer_complete(whole)`, `bots_ipc.rs:1881-1925`; DW-231) — 2 s on this row, ~100 s on the 119 s row of 07:47. The same store answers `mythomax:13b` on the Ollama provider in 0.3–3.5 s. The ring records `turn:sending` and `turn:speaking` but not the first token (`voice_log.rs:63`), so the person sees "Thinking" for half a minute and nothing counts.
4. **The model rule picked an embedding model.** `voice_target::model_for` falls back to "first offered"; on the Hermes provider that was `embeddinggemma:latest` for eight answers (Hermes' agent answered anyway; the name is wrong on the row and in the caption).
5. **The switch is in three places and no menu.** The native Bots menu and the palette's Bots category hold one item, "Toggle Answer Details" (`palette.rs:748-762`); the pane header has the metadata chip only (`bots-pane.tsx:372-378`); the phone's back bars have nothing; the switch itself is a folded band on the desktop (`bots-pane.tsx:447-454`) and a sheet on the phone (`bots-phone-pane.tsx:620-645`). The palette's Bots section is `cfg!(desktop)` (`ipc.rs:4138-4179`) although `bots` is true on the phone.

## The one sentence

**An answer cannot be stopped — the stop that exists ends the turn and not the voice, the Mac closes its ear before it speaks — and the wait for it is a blank "Thinking" that hides whose time it is.**

## Decisions this epic takes

- **AD-212 — A manual stop stops the voice.** Every "stop this answer" — the mic button, the tray, the hotkey, `keeper://voice/talk`, the pill — is `TurnEvent::Abandoned`, whose `Speaking` arm is `[StopSpeaking, ReleaseMicrophone]` and which re-arms a switched-on phrase. `(ReachAsk::Talk, Speaking) => StopSpeaking`. `Silence` stays the synthesiser's own end. On iOS `Worker::stop` and `stop_speaking` drop the `speaking` flag and start the tail gate, so the next transcript is heard.
- **AD-213 — The Mac keeps its ear open while it speaks (AD-175 revised).** `MACOS.full_duplex = true`; `voice_macos::start_capture` enables voice processing on the input node; the port gains the `speaking` flag, `SpeechDetected(text)`, the roll-before-speak, `TAIL_GATE` and `echo_dropped` — the iOS shape without the audio-session ducking. When voice processing is refused (an aggregate input device: BlackHole, Loopback — Story 22.7's measurement), the port answers half duplex with a sentence beside the switch naming the device, and the manual stop remains. `echo_dropped` in the ring is the measurement on both platforms (DW-243).
- **AD-214 — The first sentence is spoken when it arrives.** A spoken turn's answer is segmented as it streams (the boundary rule `banner::first_sentence` already has); `Sending + AnswerSentence(s)` → `Speaking, [Speak(s)]`; `Speaking + AnswerSentence(s)` → `[Enqueue(s)]`; `AnswerDone(rest)` → `[Enqueue(rest), AnswerClosed]`; the turn ends on the synthesiser's `Silence` only after `AnswerClosed`. The stop word and the manual stop cancel the queue. The ports enqueue utterances on one synthesiser; the language is chosen per sentence with the turn's first choice kept unless a sentence's detection is confident. DW-231 closes.
- **AD-215 — The wait is counted and named.** The ring records `first_token` (with `after_ms`) and `answer_closed`; the voice state line on both tiers, the banner and the pill show "Waiting for nixie · 12 s" counting up from `sent`, then "Answering". After the turn, the line says "first word after 28 s"; the caption Story 61.8 shows under metadata is the same numbers. The docs say plainly whose seconds they are: the first token is the provider's, and for Hermes it is the prefill of its own context.
- **AD-216 — A bot's speed is a fact the picker shows.** `bot_messages` already holds `ttft_ms` per answer; the voice target picker (AD-206) shows each pinned bot's median first token over its last ten answers — "nixie · first word ~25 s", "makistack ollama · ~2 s" — so choosing a fast bot for voice is a choice made with numbers. Nothing is measured that is not already stored.
- **AD-217 — The model rule never picks a model that cannot chat.** `model_for`: the last assistant model of the conversation; else the provider's own default (`hermes-agent` on Hermes); else the first offered model whose name does not say `embed`; else the refusal sentence. Rows written with an embedding model are shown as they are (history is history) and the caption names the rule.
- **AD-218 — Listening is one action in every menu that has a Bots section.** `bots-toggle-listening` in the palette registry (Bots category, offered on the phone too — the palette's `bots` gate follows `CapabilitiesVm.bots`), the native menu item, the pane header on both tiers (a chip beside the metadata chip on the desktop; the list-level back bar on the phone), the tray row — all one command, `voice_wake_set(!enabled, phrase, stop)`, state from `VoiceWakeVm`; the label is the state ("Listening on · nixie" / "Listening off"); absent where voice is unsupported (AD-27).

## What stays out
- Making Hermes faster: the prefill is nixie's system prompt, memory and skills — makistack's configuration. keeper names the seconds; it cannot spend them.
- Barge-in on any speech as the end of a turn (FR-403's shape stays: other speech asks).
- A per-sentence voice change mid-answer beyond the confidence rule.

## The failure shape this epic must not repeat
Epic 67 shipped a stop word with a test that stopped the *turn* and never checked that the *voice* stopped — the port's `stop_speaking` was reachable from exactly one effect that the manual stop never emitted. Every stop story here ends with a fake port that counts `stop_speaking` calls, and with the ring from a real answer cut mid-word on both machines.
