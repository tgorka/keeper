T1 Metaphor Mapping 11 candidates 1 survivor

Eleven rooms were tried on. Ten are struck. The decisive one was not a room at all at first: keeper is a museum job title — Keeper of Manuscripts, Keeper of Prints and Drawings, the curator-custodian of a collection who also maintains its register.

REJECTvaultLiteral fit for local-first, but a vault is for storing, not working. A vault you sit inside 14h/day is a cell.
REJECTworkshop / benchFits density. The generic maker aesthetic — wood plus orange — is a template of its own.
HOLDfield station / outpostSelf-sufficient, off-grid, keeps logs and readings. Maps hard onto local-first, sync, no telemetry.
HOLDinstrumentOscilloscope, seismograph, sonar. Precision, calibration, one glowing trace on a dark faceplate. Supplies the impressive register.
HOLDdarkroomA near-black room with one safe light; you do not open the door mid-process. Maps onto privacy, and onto incognito mode literally.
OWNEDterminalThe obvious pick, and already owned by tgsite (Night Signal). A second terminal identity would make the two products one product.
HOLDcockpit / avionicsDense readouts, low-luminance lighting, everything labelled. Density IS the aesthetic there.
HOLDlighthouse keeperThe name resonates: one person, one light, a nightly log, weather. Solitary and unglamorous in a good way.
REJECTbeekeeperA keeper of a colony of workers — the incoming bots are the colony. But a hex or insect mark says colony, not custody.
HOLDgamekeeperWalks the boundary of private land, knows every animal, admits no visitors. Exactly the local-first stance.
SURVIVORthe keeper roomNot the gallery — the back-of-house keeper room: trays, labels, accession numbers, raking light, a register with an entry for every object, conservation over display. Galleries are polished, empty and lit for visitors; workrooms are dense, labelled and lit for work. That single distinction settles the impressive-vs-usable fight before it starts.
accession numberEvery object carries a stable visible address. In keeper that is the real path or id, in mono, always in the same corner. Not decoration — navigation.
the labelA small mono uppercase tag with letterspacing on every tray. Becomes the pane-header grammar across all six surfaces.
the registerSync history and the recordings archive as dated one-line entries, ledger-ruled.
condition reportBridge health, sync health, drive present or absent. A condition report is terse and never celebratory.
provenanceGit history is provenance. Show it as chain-of-custody, not as a devtool.
raking lightConservators light objects from a low angle to reveal surface. Translates to depth by a single 1px top-edge highlight, never a drop shadow on inline elements.
reversibilityA conservator only makes interventions that can be undone. keeper never mutates files destructively; no irreversible action without a named undo.
trays and drawersThe pane and panel strip IS the tray rack. Panels slide, are labelled, and are never modal.

The blend held in tension: keeper-room (custody, labels, register, raking light) × instrument panel (readouts, calibration ticks, one lit lamp). Custody supplies the meaning; the instrument supplies the impressiveness.

SO

the brief, restated The design brief is not “invent a personality”. It is: give the pixels the voice the copy already has. Terse, exact, unshowy, never cheerful, always names the specific thing. That is the whole aesthetic spec.

T4 Assumption Reversal 4 reversals green resolved

Four assumptions about the colour green, each flipped until it became a rule.

ASSUMPTION Greenish means terminal phosphor.
→
REVERSED Greenish means the green of things that are KEPT — verdigris on a copper roof, moss on stone, the baize of a desk, the green of a bank ledger, a chalkboard, a museum label, British racing green.
ASSUMPTION Green must be the accent.
→
REVERSED Make green the GROUND — a green-black surface like baize or a chalkboard. It instantly stops looking like every dark-grey app. Cost, and it is real: keeper is also a file browser with image, PDF and video viewers, and a tinted ground casts on every preview. So the ground must be barely green — enough chroma to read as material, not enough to colour-cast.
ASSUMPTION Green means OK / success.
→
REVERSED — THE RULE Green is the brand, therefore green cannot mean OK. Healthy is never coloured: it is a lit neutral lamp plus text-dim. Only degraded (amber) and broken (red) spend colour. This collides head-on with --bridge-healthy #16a34a (src/index.css:95) and --swipe-read mapping to --primary (src/index.css:105) — so it retires them. Checkable by script. Corollary: colour in keeper only ever marks a deviation or the user’s own attention.
ASSUMPTION Warm greens are unfashionable.
→
REVERSED That is the point. Hue 130-ish yellow-greens are rare in software and read as material — lichen, moss, sap — rather than as digital. Naming it lichen is not a mood word: lichen grows only in clean air, grows slowly, grows on stone, and is used to DATE surfaces. A keeper’s organism, and the story survives being said out loud to an engineer.

M The mark 24 grid / stroke 1.25 visor = state display

A visored sentinel head — squared, slightly wider than tall, near-square corners, ONE horizontal visor slot, no eyes, no mouth, no antenna, no neck. The visor is the only place the accent appears, so the rest is a single flat shape — which is exactly what a macOS template tray icon needs.

Why this shape

  • The visor is a READOUT, not an eye slot. The head is a small dark panel with one lit band, which ties the mark to the instrument half of the metaphor.
  • The head is the app’s own primitive. Same rectangle as a pane, at 1:1.
  • One asymmetry so it cannot be a template: a single hallmark notch bitten out of the upper-left corner — the keeper’s punch mark on the underside of the object. Repeated exactly once in the UI, as the top-left corner cut of the launch window. One motif, two places, never more.
  • No face. Faces promise personality; keeper promises custody.
  • Rail glyphs and the mark are ONE custom family drawn on a 20 grid at stroke 1.25 to the mark’s geometry. lucide is permitted only below rail level, and never in the same row as a custom glyph.

Killer detail: the visor is a state display

  • One glyph carries the whole tray family, and the mark and the status system become the same object.
  • The same four states run across mark, tray, bot list and sync indicator. A bot that is running shows the visor mid-state; a bot that is waiting shows it unlit.
  • These four must be specified before the tray icons are redrawn: the repo already ships tray-idle / tray-sync / tray-recording / tray-error-template.png, and that family is the mark’s real production constraint — it has to survive as a 22px monochrome template.

T6 TRIZ Contradiction 3 separations resolved in space
Impressive CONTRADICTS Usable

Stated exactly: impressive normally buys attention with large type, whitespace, imagery and motion. A dense all-day tool can afford none of the four. Every gram spent on impression is taken from information.

Separation in space — the resolution

Character lives entirely in the CHROME — rail, pane headers, status band, command palette, tray, launch, about, empty states — and is banned from the CONTENT: message list, note body, file table. The frame is where the app is beautiful; the content is deliberately boring.

  • The content rule that makes it hold: the accent may appear in content only as a 2px edge bar, a selection, or a caret. Never a fill, never a background, never a badge.
  • Second-order benefit: banning character from content makes every future feature cheap to design. A new surface inherits the frame and only has to solve its own table.

Separation in time

The app is silent while you work and expressive at transitions — launch, first paint of a surface, the empty state. Impression lands in the moments nobody is reading.

  • Empty states are the one place a dense app has room, and keeper already writes real sentences for them (src/components/layout/panel-strip.tsx:49, :71, :77, :80, :91). Give those sentences a stage: generous space, the mono face set larger, the mark at low contrast.
  • The launch window and the about panel can be lavish, because each is seen once and neither competes with data.

Separation in scale

Be impressive at 1px. In a dense tool the only uncontested real estate is the 1-4px gutter, so put the identity there: hairlines, tick marks, a corner notch, a 2px accent bar.

  • Calibration ticks along the pane divider — a vertical rule with small marks at a fixed interval. Costs one pixel of width, says instrument immediately, and no template ships it.
  • Density itself as the flex: publish the row height as a token, put everything on a 4px grid, and let rigour be the impression. People call perfectly aligned dense information “fast” even when it is not.

Minus

A green-tinted ground fights the file and media viewers. Mitigation Already a rule — chroma capped at 0.014, and viewers render on a strictly neutral plate inside the tinted chrome.
The frame-carries-character split is only as good as its enforcement; without a check script it decays. Mitigation Every refusal was written to be greppable — hex literals, hue ranges, backdrop-filter, font names, the sparkle codepoint.

Interesting

Focus-by-light rather than focus-by-border is the only genuinely novel interaction detail in the set — and also the riskiest for accessibility. It must be additive to a real focus ring, never a replacement.

Banishing character from the content areas means the app’s beauty is carried by roughly 4% of its pixels. If the chrome is executed at 90% instead of 100%, the result is not a slightly-less-impressive app — it is an ordinary app with an odd green tint.
