- (technique by coach) started Metaphor Mapping
- (idea by coach) Vault: literal fit for local-first, but a vault is for storing not working; a vault you sit inside 14h/day is a cell. Reject as primary.
- (idea by coach) Workshop/bench: tools on a rail, work half-done, everything in arm's reach. Fits density, but generic maker aesthetic (wood + orange) is a template of its own.
- (idea by coach) Field station / remote outpost: self-sufficient, off-grid, keeps logs and readings. Maps hard onto local-first + sync + no telemetry.
- (idea by coach) Instrument (oscilloscope, seismograph, sonar): precision, calibration, one glowing trace on a dark faceplate. Supplies the impressive register.
- (idea by coach) Darkroom: near-black room with one safe light; you do not open the door mid-process. Maps onto privacy and onto incognito mode literally.
- (idea by coach) Terminal: the obvious pick, and already owned by tgsite (Night Signal). Refuse - a second terminal identity would make the two products one product.
- (idea by coach) Cockpit / avionics night panel: dense readouts, low-luminance lighting, everything labelled. Density IS the aesthetic there.
- (idea by coach) Lighthouse keeper: the name resonates - one person, one light, a nightly log, weather. Solitary and unglamorous in a good way.
- (idea by coach) Beekeeper: a keeper of a colony of workers - the incoming bots are the colony. Kept, named, given a job; not friends.
- (idea by coach) Gamekeeper: walks the boundary of private land, knows every animal, admits no visitors. Exactly the local-first stance.
- (insight by coach) the decisive one: KEEPER IS A MUSEUM JOB TITLE. Keeper of Manuscripts, Keeper of Prints and Drawings - the curator-custodian of a collection who also maintains its register.
- (idea by coach) So the metaphor is not the gallery, it is the BACK-OF-HOUSE KEEPER ROOM: trays, labels, accession numbers, raking light, a register with an entry for every object, conservation over display.
- (idea by coach) Galleries are polished, empty and lit for visitors. Workrooms are dense, labelled and lit for work. keeper is a workroom - that single distinction settles the impressive-vs-usable fight before it starts.
- (idea by coach) Metaphor extension - accession number: every object in the collection carries a stable visible address. In keeper that is the real path/id, shown in mono, always in the same corner. Not decoration, navigation.
- (idea by coach) Metaphor extension - the label: a small mono uppercase tag with letterspacing on every tray. Becomes the pane-header grammar across all six surfaces.
- (idea by coach) Metaphor extension - the register / daybook: sync history and the recordings archive rendered as dated one-line entries, ledger-ruled.
- (idea by coach) Metaphor extension - condition report: bridge health, sync health, drive present/absent. A condition report is terse and never celebratory.
- (idea by coach) Metaphor extension - provenance: git history is provenance. Show it as chain-of-custody, not as a devtool.
- (idea by coach) Metaphor extension - raking light: conservators light objects from a low angle to reveal surface. Translate to depth by a single 1px top-edge highlight, never a drop shadow on inline elements.
- (idea by coach) Metaphor extension - reversibility: a conservator only makes interventions that can be undone. Maps to keeper never mutating the user's files destructively, and to UI: no irreversible action without a named undo.
- (idea by coach) Metaphor extension - trays and drawers: the pane/panel strip IS the tray rack. Panels slide, are labelled, and are never modal.
- (idea by coach) Blend to hold in tension: keeper-room (custody, labels, register, raking light) x instrument panel (readouts, calibration ticks, one lit lamp). Custody supplies the meaning, the instrument supplies the impressiveness.
- (decision by coach) primary metaphor candidate: THE KEEPER ROOM - the back-of-house workroom of a collection, at night, lit by one lamp.

- (idea by coach) Strange: the one semantic colour that is not grey is --incognito #6d28d9 / #a78bfa (index.css:92, 172). Of all hues available, privacy was painted the exact violet every AI product of 2026 uses.
- (idea by coach) Strange: --background is oklch(1 0 0) - pure white (index.css:67). No tool someone stares at for fourteen hours should have a pure white ground.
- (idea by coach) Strange: --radius is 10px (index.css:109) on a surface whose list rows are ~28px tall. The corner is a third of the row. The app is rounder than it is tall.
- (idea by coach) Strange: --font-heading is literally var(--font-sans) (index.css:8) and --font-sans is the OS UI stack (index.css:9). There is no heading voice, no machine voice, no typographic opinion at all.
- (idea by coach) Strange: chart-1..5 are five greys (index.css:85-89) so DATA has no colour, while a bridge badge does (index.css:95-97). Colour is spent on chrome and withheld from content - exactly backwards.
- (idea by coach) Strange: the only saturated palette in the app is the 8-hue account wheel (index.css:136-143), evenly spaced round the circle. It is a rainbow by arithmetic, not by choice, and hues 130/170 collide with the brand green while 275/320 are the banned violet-magenta.
- (idea by coach) Strange: the app icon is a white speech bubble on a green rounded square. The product is chat + notes + files + recordings + sync. The mark describes one sixth of the app, and describes it in the visual language of every messenger ever shipped.
- (idea by coach) Strange: the nav is thirteen lucide imports (sidebar-pane.tsx:1-15) - MessageSquare, Archive, Inbox, Radio, Video, Film, FolderSync, FolderTree, NotebookPen, Settings. The same glyph set as every other product of the era, at the same stroke weight.
- (idea by coach) Strange: up to ten nav rows, all icon+label, all equal weight (sidebar-pane.tsx:49-83). Nothing in the chrome says which surface is THE surface, so the app has no centre of gravity.
- (insight by coach) the real find: keeper already HAS a voice - in its prose. panel-strip.tsx:49 'Nothing is open here yet. Click a file to open it.' :77 'The folder this file was in is no longer set up in keeper.' :91 'keeper cannot show a recording in a panel yet.' :121 names the missing file. Full sentences, lowercase brand, no error codes, no marketing adjectives.
- (insight by coach) so the design brief is not 'invent a personality'. It is: give the pixels the voice the copy already has. Terse, exact, unshowy, never cheerful, always names the specific thing. That is the whole aesthetic spec, restated.
- (idea by coach) Corollary: the identity must be checkable the way the copy is consistent. Anything that cannot be enforced by a script will drift back to shadcn defaults inside two epics - the violet at index.css:190 is the proof.
- (idea by coach) Strange: the app is capability-honest (entries are absent, not disabled, sidebar-pane.tsx:43-48, 77-81) - a strong product principle with no visual expression yet. Absence should look deliberate, not like a gap.
- (technique by coach) started Concept Blending - the mark
- (idea by coach) Default robot head = rounded square, two dot eyes, maybe an antenna. That is the Claude/ChatGPT/every-AI-startup face. It is the speech bubble again, one trend later. Refuse.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x helmet visor: one horizontal slot, no eyes, no mouth. A visor has no expression and cannot be chirpy. Reads as robot instantly.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x instrument faceplate: the visor is not an eye slot, it is a READOUT. The head is a small dark panel with one lit band. Ties the mark to the instrument half of the metaphor.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x accession label: a head with a tag - too fussy at 16px. Keep the idea, spend it on the UI label grammar instead.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x Stevenson screen (the louvred white instrument shelter at a weather station): a slotted head. Beautiful, but louvres die below 32px.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x lighthouse lantern room: the head IS the lamp. Its light is the accent. Merges mark and accent colour into one story.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x strike plate: 'keeper' is also hardware - the metal plate a bolt slides into, and the loop that holds a belt tail. Both are the part that HOLDS. A single notch in the mark can carry that.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x pane: the head is the same rectangle as the app's panels, so the mark is literally the app's primitive at 1:1.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x bracket pair: a head drawn as [ ] with a rule between. Purely typographic, sets beautifully in mono, but too clever and unreadable as an app icon at 32px.
- (idea by coach) Blend robot head x Easter Island moai: monolithic, silent, standing watch. Impressive and un-chirpy, but too mythic for a utility.
- (idea by coach) Rejected blends: robot x leaf/seed (soft, ecological, wrong stance), robot x hexagon (crypto), robot x bee (charming, but a hex/insect mark says colony not custody).
- (decision by coach) mark direction: A VISORED SENTINEL HEAD - squared, slightly wider than tall, near-square corners (2px on a 24 grid), ONE horizontal visor slot, no eyes, no mouth, no antenna, no neck.
- (idea by coach) The visor is the only place the accent appears in the mark, so the rest is a single flat shape - which is exactly what a macOS template tray icon needs, and the repo already ships a tray family (tray-idle/sync/recording/error-template.png).
- (idea by coach) Killer detail: the visor is a STATE DISPLAY. Full bar = idle. Broken/dashed bar = syncing. Short centred bar = error. Filled dot = recording. One glyph, the whole tray family, and the mark and the status system become the same object.
- (idea by coach) One asymmetry so it cannot be a template: a single hallmark notch bitten out of the upper-left corner - the keeper's punch mark on the underside of the object.
- (insight by coach) the mark has no face because keeper's position is that your AI is a kept instrument, not a friend. Faces promise personality; keeper promises custody. The refusal to draw eyes IS the brand statement, and it pre-answers the bots surface.
- (idea by coach) Anti-list for the mark: no antennae, no ears, no rivets, no bolts, no smile arc, no two dots, no 3/4 perspective render, no gloss highlight, no gradient body, no blink-on-typing idle animation.
- (technique by coach) started Assumption Reversal - green
- (idea by coach) Assumption: greenish means terminal phosphor. Reverse: greenish means the green of things that are KEPT - verdigris on a copper roof, moss on stone, the baize of a desk, the green of a bank ledger, a chalkboard, a museum label, British racing green.
- (idea by coach) Phosphor #00ff00 is obvious pick #1 and it is retro cosplay; tgsite already bans phosphor glow and scanlines for the same reason (DESIGN.md:124). Refuse.
- (idea by coach) Acid lime / chartreuse is obvious pick #2 - the loudest colour trend of 2025-26. Fashionable now means dated in eighteen months and template-shaped today. Refuse.
- (idea by coach) Emerald #10b981 is Tailwind emerald-500 - literally the framework default. Using it is indistinguishable from not choosing. Refuse.
- (idea by coach) The current dark brand #3ecfae (index.css:153) is not green, it is mint/aqua at hue ~175 - the fintech-SaaS teal. And the light brand #0f6e5c (index.css:73) is a deep teal. The app is already teal, not green. Retire both.
- (idea by coach) Assumption: green must be the accent. Reverse: make green the GROUND - a green-black surface like baize or a chalkboard. Instantly stops looking like every dark-grey app.
- (idea by coach) Cost of a green ground, and it is real: keeper is also a file browser with image/PDF/video viewers. A tinted ground casts on every preview and screenshot. So the ground must be barely green - enough chroma to read as material, not enough to colour-cast.
- (decision by coach) two greens with different jobs, which is exactly the workroom pairing: a near-black GROUND with a trace of green (the baize) and a bright warm-green LAMP (the accent).
- (idea by coach) Assumption: green means OK/success. This collides head-on: --bridge-healthy is #16a34a (index.css:95) and --swipe-read maps to --primary (index.css:105). If green is the brand, a healthy app is uniformly brand-coloured and the accent stops meaning anything.
- (insight by coach) so the rule has to be: GREEN IS THE BRAND, THEREFORE GREEN CANNOT MEAN OK. Healthy is never coloured - it is a lit neutral lamp plus text-dim. Only degraded (amber) and broken (red) spend colour. This is checkable by script and it retires #16a34a.
- (idea by coach) Corollary: colour in keeper only ever marks a deviation or the user's own attention. That is a condition-report grammar, straight out of the metaphor.
- (idea by coach) Assumption: warm greens are unfashionable. Reverse: that is the point - hue 130ish yellow-greens are rare in software and read as material (lichen, moss, sap) rather than as digital.
- (decision by coach) computed accent: oklch(0.80 0.15 132) = #99d267 - lichen. 10.62:1 on the ground, so it is text-grade, not just decoration.
- (idea by coach) Naming it lichen is not a mood word: lichen grows only in clean air, grows slowly, grows on stone, and is used to DATE surfaces. A keeper's organism, and the story survives being said out loud to an engineer.
- (idea by coach) Computed ground family, all at hue 155 chroma 0.014 so the greys are the same material as the accent: ground #0c120e, raised #171e19, overlay #222a25, border #2d3530.
- (idea by coach) Computed ink: text #e1e9e2 (15.3:1), text-dim #99a19a (7.14:1 - so it can legally carry every timestamp, path and count), text-faint #636b65 (3.45:1, decorative only, never information).
- (idea by coach) Light theme is PAPER, not white: #f4f8f3 ground, #eaeee9 raised, #131a14 ink (16.5:1), accent deepened to #396d0a (5.81:1 on paper). Same room by daylight, not a second design.
- (idea by coach) Giving every neutral a chroma of 0.014 instead of 0 is the single cheapest move in the whole redesign. It costs one search-and-replace and it is the difference between 'a themed shadcn app' and 'a designed app'.
- (idea by coach) Amber stays, but only as status (--held #b45309 / degraded #d97706, index.css:90,96). Amber is tgsite's brand colour; keeper must not take it as one, or the two products become the same product.
- (idea by coach) Red budget, keeper's own version of tgsite's (DESIGN.md:138-141): red appears in exactly two roles - the recording lamp (--recording-red, index.css:94) and destructive confirmation. Nowhere else, ever.
- (idea by coach) The violet must be exterminated by name, and the names exist: index.css:92, :172 (incognito), :190 (the unused shadcn sidebar-primary), :141 and :203 (account-hue-5 at 275), :142 and :204 (account-hue-6 at 320).
- (idea by coach) Replacement for the violet incognito: the room goes to SAFELIGHT. In incognito the accent disappears entirely, everything drops to text-dim, and the pane header carries a 45-degree hatch band. Diegetic to the darkroom, zero purple, and it reads at a glance from across the desk.
- (idea by coach) Account hues stay - eight accounts genuinely need telling apart - but pulled off the rainbow: drop violet and magenta, cut chroma, and add a second axis (solid / dashed / double 3px edge bar) so the set survives colour-blindness and monochrome printing.
- (technique by coach) started Sensory Exploration
- (idea by coach) Touch: anodised aluminium and paper. Never glass. Glass is glassmorphism is backdrop-filter is the single most 2026 material there is.
- (idea by coach) Light: one raking source, upper-left. Depth is a 1px top-edge highlight at ~6% white. Drop shadows exist only on things that genuinely float - dialog, command palette, tooltip, context menu. Nothing inline casts a shadow.
- (idea by coach) Tooth: a fine grain overlay at ~2% on chrome surfaces only - never over a media viewer, a video frame or a PDF, where it would look like a compression artefact. tgsite grains the whole viewport (DESIGN.md:200-203); keeper cannot, and the difference is a real product constraint, not a preference.
- (idea by coach) Temperature: the app should taste like strong tea, not an energy drink. Operationally: no fill ever uses the accent at full chroma across an area larger than a button.
- (idea by coach) Sound: keeper is silent. The only audible event in the product is recording start/stop, because that one has legal weight.
- (idea by coach) Weight: the chrome is heavy and the content is light. Hairline borders at 8-10% opacity and FEW of them - structure carried by ink hierarchy and alignment, not by boxing everything.
- (idea by coach) Typography, the biggest single win: two faces. One proportional for prose and labels, one MONO for every machine-generated string - paths, ids, timestamps, byte sizes, durations, hashes, git refs, room ids. keeper is full of these and currently sets them all in the OS UI font (index.css:8-9).
- (idea by coach) The mono face is the accession number made visible. It is also the exact typographic move the metaphor demands: the label is printed, the note is handwritten.
- (idea by coach) Micro-label: mono, uppercase, ~10px, +0.08em tracking, text-dim. That is the specimen tag, and it becomes the pane-header grammar on all six surfaces. Costs 14px of height and buys the whole identity.
- (idea by coach) Tabular lining figures everywhere. In a tool that shows sizes, durations, counts and dates in columns, tabular figures are simultaneously a usability fix and the most legible signal that someone designed this.
- (idea by coach) Face choice must dodge the era: Inter is the single most template-signalling typeface of the decade. Ban it by name. tgsite already pairs JetBrains Mono + Space Grotesk (DESIGN.md:44,60) - keeper should share the mono family for kinship and take a different proportional face so they are siblings, not clones.
- (idea by coach) Test the whole thing in one colour: print a screenshot in greyscale. If the hierarchy survives, colour is doing its proper job (marking deviation and attention) and nothing else.
- (technique by coach) started TRIZ Contradiction - impressive vs usable
- (idea by coach) The contradiction, stated exactly: impressive normally buys attention with large type, whitespace, imagery and motion. A dense all-day tool can afford none of the four. Every gram spent on impression is taken from information.
- (idea by coach) TRIZ separation in SPACE, and this is the resolution: character lives entirely in the CHROME - rail, pane headers, status band, command palette, tray, launch, about, empty states - and is banned from the CONTENT - message list, note body, file table. The frame is where the app is beautiful; the content is deliberately boring.
- (idea by coach) The content rule that makes it hold: the accent may appear in content only as a 2px edge bar, a selection, or a caret. Never as a fill, never as a background, never as a badge.
- (idea by coach) TRIZ separation in TIME: the app is silent while you work and expressive at transitions - launch, first paint of a surface, the empty state. Impression lands in the moments nobody is reading.
- (idea by coach) TRIZ separation in SCALE: be impressive at 1px. In a dense tool the only uncontested real estate is the 1-4px gutter, so put the identity there - hairlines, tick marks, a corner notch, a 2px accent bar.
- (idea by coach) Concrete: calibration ticks along the pane divider. A vertical rule with small marks at a fixed interval. Costs one pixel of width, says instrument immediately, and no template ships it.
- (idea by coach) Concrete: the pane header as a label strip - mono uppercase name, hairline, count in tabular figures, right-aligned state. Repeated across six surfaces it stops being decoration and becomes a system.
- (idea by coach) Concrete: a status band at the very bottom, one line, mono, showing vault, branch, sync state and the active bot. Zero pixels when there is nothing to say - tgsite's console dock rule (DESIGN.md:224-227) applied to a different problem.
- (idea by coach) Motion budget: exactly ONE animating element on screen at any time, and it is always the sync/activity indicator. Everything else is a =<160ms state transition. Written as a number, it is enforceable in review.
- (insight by coach) empty states are the one place a dense app has room, and keeper already writes real sentences for them (panel-strip.tsx:49, 71, 77, 80, 91). Give those sentences a stage - generous space, the mono face set larger, the mark at low contrast. Impressive and useful at once, because it is the only screen with nothing to crowd.
- (idea by coach) Density itself as the flex: publish the row height as a token, put everything on a 4px grid, and let rigour be the impression. People find perfectly aligned dense information impressive; they call it 'fast' even when it is not.
- (idea by coach) The launch window and the about panel can be lavish, because each is seen once and neither competes with data.
- (idea by coach) Named risk of the frame/content split: it can read as two designs bolted together. Mitigation is material continuity - the same chroma-0.014 neutrals, the same hairline weight and the same 4px grid run through both; only the ink density changes.
- (idea by coach) Second-order benefit: banning character from content makes every future feature cheap to design. A new surface inherits the frame and only has to solve its own table.
