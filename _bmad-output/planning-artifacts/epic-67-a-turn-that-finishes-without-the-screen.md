## What was measured

1. **The turn is Rust's until it has heard, and the webview's after.** `keeper_core::voice::Turn::perform` treats `Effect::SendText` as a no-op (`voice/mod.rs:561`); the send is `BotVoiceMic`'s effect (`bot-voice-mic.tsx:135-146` → `onHeard` → `botsChatSend`, `bots-phone-pane.tsx:587-594`), and the speak is the pane's reaction to the stream's `closed` event (`speakIfHeard`, `bot-voice-mic.tsx:116-124`, called at `bots-pane.tsx:181-183`). On the phone this JS runs only while a Bots conversation is the pushed level (`phone-shell.tsx:990-1009`, unmount at `:229-231`). WebKit keeps the WebContent process runnable only for a visible view, a page that itself plays audio, or a page that itself captures (`WebPageProxy.cpp:3823-3893`); native `AVAudioEngine`/`AVSpeechSynthesizer` in the Rust host sets neither flag, and the screen lock releases network activity early (`WebPageProxyIOS.mm:593-610`). So with another app in front or the screen locked the turn parks in `Heard` — no silence budget there (`turn.rs:240-245`) — until the pane's JS runs again. That is "I hear the answer only when I open the app", exactly.
2. **Speech itself is fine in the background.** Apple: `AVSpeechSynthesizer` "obeys the same rules as other audio" (forum 737157); with `UIBackgroundModes: audio` and a session activated in the foreground it speaks with the screen locked. keeper's session is `.playAndRecord`, activated when the phrase is armed (`voice_ios.rs:1368-1385`), `mixWithOthers` while armed and `duckOthers` while speaking (`:1119-1134`). Nothing here needs fixing; it has simply never been reached from the background.
3. **The lock screen has one surface and the build cannot draw it.** `voice_ipc::push` fans out to the island alone (`voice_ipc.rs:330-333`); `voice_island.rs` records `unsupported` when the extension is absent and shows nothing else; no voice path calls `Platform::notify` (`ipc.rs:900-914`, `UNUserNotificationCenter`, permission asked at boot `lib.rs:637-654`). Apple: a local notification with no trigger is "delivered right away", from any thread, and when the app is not in front "the system interacts with the user for you"; `.active` lights the screen and shows a banner over the app in front; the same identifier replaces the previous one in place. No entitlement, no App ID, nothing the free team lacks. Now Playing is not a surface for a mixable session.
4. **Barge-in exists as "any speech opens a new question", and the word is thrown away.** `result_handler` turns any non-empty transcript while the `speaking` flag is up into `TurnEvent::SpeechDetected` without its text (`voice_ios.rs:1074-1077`); `(Speaking, SpeechDetected)` → `Listening`, effects `[StopSpeaking, OpenMicrophone]` (`turn.rs:195-200`); a phrase is matched only in `Idle` (`mod.rs:436-442`). So "stop" today stops the answer and then waits for a question that never comes. Echo: the session mode is `AVAudioSessionModeDefault` with `defaultToSpeaker` (`:1368-1385`, `:1119`) and VPIO on the input node (`:1461-1463`); Apple documents that VPIO sets `voiceChat` implicitly, and a 2026 field report measures `.default` + `.defaultToSpeaker` as the combination that leaves the app hearing its own tail — an ~800 ms post-utterance gate was what worked. The owner hears whole answers in the foreground, so the tail is not stopping keeper today; whether it does in a quiet room with the screen locked is what the ring will say.

## The one sentence

**The turn is finished by a screen that is not there — so when keeper is not in front it hears you, and then waits for you to come and look.**

## Decisions this epic takes

- **AD-205 — The hands-free turn is Rust's from the phrase to the last word.** `Effect::SendText` is performed by the shell: `voice_ipc::transition` sees it and calls `bots_ipc::send_spoken(text)`, which opens the stream the way `bots_chat_send` does; the stream's close for a spoken turn calls `voice_ipc::answer_complete(text)`, which drives `Speak`. The webview observes through the events it already has (`voiceWatch`, the stream events) and drives nothing: `speakIfHeard` and the `onHeard → botsChatSend` hand-off go. A button-started turn takes the same path — one turn, not two — with the heard text landing in the conversation exactly as before.
- **AD-206 — The voice has a target chosen on the screen, never inferred at three in the morning.** A spoken turn goes to `bots.voice_target`: the bot (and its current conversation, or a new one on that bot) the person picked in the Bots voice section, on both tiers. Unset, it is the pinned bot that was most recently talked to; if there is none, the turn is refused with a sentence ("choose a bot to talk to under Bots") shown where the refusal beside the switch is shown (AD-190) and recorded in the ring. No target is ever guessed from what is open on the screen.
- **AD-207 — When keeper is not in front, the words go to the lock screen.** On every island-word change (`Word::Heard/Thinking/Answering/Speaking/Failed`) while the app is not active — `UIApplication.applicationState` read on the main thread, never the JS lifecycle — `Platform::notify` posts a local notification with a fixed identifier (replaced in place): "Heard: …" with the words, "Thinking", "Answer: …" with the first sentence, "Listening stopped: …" with the reason; `.active`, no sound (the answer is the sound), cleared when the turn ends or the app comes to front. In front, nothing is posted. The island (AD-194) stays the richer surface where it can be signed; the notification is the one every free-team build has.
- **AD-208 — "Stop" ends the turn; other speech still asks.** `SpeechDetected` carries its transcript. `Turn::apply` matches a stop phrase — `bots.stop_phrase`, default "stop", one word in the recognition locale, parsed by `WakePhrase::parse` and matched by `matches` — and on a match performs `[StopSpeaking, ReleaseMicrophone]` into `Idle` and re-arms the wake phrase (AD-190); speech that is not the stop phrase keeps FR-403's shape (stop, then listen for a question). The stop word is shown beside the wake phrase in the voice section on both tiers.
- **AD-209 — The port's own voice is kept out of its ears by measurement, not by hope.** The session mode is set to `voiceChat` explicitly (Apple: the mode VPIO is tuned for), `defaultToSpeaker` kept; after every utterance ends the port drops transcripts for a settle window (`TAIL_GATE`, 800 ms, a constant with the field report as its source) before `Silence` may be reported; every transcript dropped by the gate is a ring event (`echo_dropped`, with the words) so the run on kalypso says whether the app hears itself and what it heard.
- **AD-210 — What is claimed is what the ring recorded on hardware.** Acceptance for 67.1–67.3 is a run on kalypso with the screen locked: the phrase, a question, the banner, the spoken answer, "stop" mid-answer, and the ring afterwards naming `wake_matched`, `sent`, `answer`, `speaking`, `stop_matched` (and any `echo_dropped`) with timestamps. Copy that says "notifies only while open" is corrected where a voice banner would contradict it (`no-background-sync-disclosure.tsx:23-25`, the disclosure's first line).
- **AD-211 — No new destination.** The bot endpoint is the one already disclosed; the notification is local; `docs/egress.md` says so in one sentence.

## What stays out
- The island itself: its story is 65.5, blocked on an Apple ID in hesperia's Xcode; this epic gives every build the banner.
- Push notifications, a Now Playing card, Siri/App Intents.
- Barge-in on any speech onset as the *end* of a turn: the stop word ends it; any other speech asks, as FR-403 said.
- A stop word that is not in the recogniser's vocabulary (on-device recognition ignores contextual strings); "stop" is a word in every listening locale keeper offers.

## The failure shape this epic must not repeat
Epic 62 designed the turn with the screen as a participant and nobody noticed, because on the Mac the screen is always there. Every story here ends with a run on kalypso with the screen locked, and the ring — not a test through a fake — is the evidence that the turn finished.
