## The one sentence

**keeper can ask a person before a bot writes, but only while the window is open and the thread waits; it forgets the ask on restart, refuses every ask when nobody is watching, and cannot tell which device a "yes" came from.** The fix is four parts:
- **Tiers.** A pure rule gives every call T0–T5, one tier higher when the work was handed on, unattended, tainted, or reached through a KVM.
- **The record.** An action that waits is an immutable file bound by a digest to exactly what will run.
- **Park and resume.** The run stops and holds nothing; after a verified decision the owning host consumes it exactly once — by an event the homeserver orders before anything runs — and continues, after a restart or a takeover too.
- **The card.** The exact payload and keeper's own summary, in every keeper client of the people who may decide; only a verified device's answer counts, and an irreversible action is decided only by the person who asked, from another device than the host's own.

## Open questions for the coordinator

Each has the reading this plan builds to. None is resolved silently.

- **Q1. The claim-epoch precondition after a takeover.** The record carried `preconditions.claim_epoch`, and AD-394 re-checked "the claim epoch" before consuming, while FR-797 says the run continues "after a restart or a takeover too". **Ruled (R25: a claim epoch is not an approval precondition; R29 F7):** the record holds no claim epoch, in `preconditions` or anywhere digested. At consume time the consuming host must hold the session's current claim (it re-reads the claim before it sends the consumed event); that is a check, not a bound field, so a takeover is not drift, and a host that has lost the claim does not consume.
- **Q2. Who decides a shared session's approval.** AD-395: the sender must be a reader of the label and listed in `approvers`. **Plan's reading:** `approvers` = the label's readers; below T4 the first valid decision wins and later ones are ignored and logged; **at T4 only the requester — the person at the head of the record's `dispatch_chain` — decides** (R28 S-28), so a T4 action on one person's resources is never decided by the other reader. A two-person rule is DW-379.
- **Q3. The rung below the card.** The architecture's rungs put 93.1, 93.2 and 93.4 in `epic93-approvals` below `epic93-card` (93.3), which brings the only way a decision arrives. A rung that parks runs nobody can approve would turn today's refusal into an hour- or day-long wait that ends in a denial. **Plan's reading:** `HostRuntime` parks only when a decision source is installed (93.3's `MatrixApprover`); without one, an action that needs a person is refused with `UNATTENDED_REFUSAL` exactly as before, so `epic93-approvals` merged alone changes no behaviour.
- **Q4. The card's verdict.** The request is a timeline item; its decision, consumption or expiry arrive later as other events, which matrix-sdk-ui does not aggregate. **Plan's reading:** as 91.1's header, the card's state is projected beside the stream, keyed by the approval id, from the decision events, the `consumed` event (Q10) and the status anchor's `detail`.
- **Q5. Tiers the table does not name.** AD-392 lists no row for `delegate`, `reply` or `card_update` on the session's own card. **Plan's reading:** `delegate` T1 (its guards are the label and the bounds, 92.1, and every action in the child is raised); `reply` into the session's own room T1; `card_update` on a card of the session's own T1 and of another session T2 (AD-392's "card changes in another session"); a message into a room with a person outside the requester's `dispatch_chain` T3. **Ruled (R28 S-21):** `card_update` that sets `schedule` or `workflow`, and a `delegate` whose `card` carries either, are T3 at every session kind, whatever the card.
- **Q6. Canonical JSON.** `binding_digest` is "SHA-256 of the RFC 8785 canonical JSON", and no RFC 8785 implementation is in the lock. **Ruled (R25: an RFC 8785 subset in core, no floats in digested fields; R29 F11):** `keeper_core::agents::approval::canonical` sorts object keys by their UTF-16 code units, writes strings with RFC 8785's escapes (§3.2.2.2), and writes integers only: a float anywhere in the digested object refuses the record at creation, naming its JSON path. No float form is implemented, because nothing digested may hold one. The digested object is `{id, session, agent, tool, args, exec_binding, checkpoint.sha256, preconditions}` (R28 S-24: the record's own id, session and agent are bound, so a decision can never be paired with another record of identical action bytes). No crate is added.
- **Q7. Where a declassification is decided.** AD-391: "decided through their proxy's DM". **Plan's reading:** a `declassify` request goes to each approver's proxy DM (92.6's route), never to the session room, whose other members are what the block protects against.
- **Q8. "every device".** FR-798 said the card appears "on every device of the people who may decide"; the lock screen and push are 98.1, Android 98.3. **Ruled (R29 F14):** FR-798 now says what the repo proves — the card in the session room on every keeper client of the people who may decide, proved on the desktop against the Synapse harness — and the phone's card is gated by a device run recorded in `docs/agents.md` § *Measured* (acceptance 8). A closed phone learns of a card in 98.1.
- **Q9. The desktop's trust anchor.** AD-395 (*Ambiguities* 4): on the desktop, "the signed-in person's own verified identity". The in-process agent copy has its own device and crypto store. **Ruled (R28 S-06, R29 F3: no trust on first use; keeper never pins by itself):** the desktop host writes no pin. It accepts a decision from the signed-in person only while matrix-sdk reports that person's own identity verified on this device (`UserIdentity::is_verified`, `identities/users.rs:407`) — a state the person reaches by their own comparison in keeper's verification flow — and compares the deciding device's owner master key with that identity's; a reset of cross-signing turns it unverified, and decisions stop until the person verifies again (the card says so). A headless host accepts a person only once a person has written `[[trust]].master_key` after comparing fingerprints. **And (R28 S-22):** a T4 decision on a session the desktop hosts must come from a device that is not this process — the person's phone, or another keeper — because here the approving client and the agent host share one process; the card says why.
- **Q10. Consumed once, across hosts** (R28 S-01, ruled: the consume is an event the homeserver orders before any effect). **Plan's reading:** before running anything, the owning host sends `dev.keeper.agent.approval.consumed {id, epoch, host}` into the session room and waits for the server's event id; it then reads the room back around that event (`/context` on it) and runs the action only if its own event is the first `consumed` event for that id in the room's order. The log's `approval {state: consumed, matrix_event}` line is the local mirror, written after the event is accepted and before the effect. A host resuming the session — at start, after a takeover, on a decision file — reads the room first: any `consumed` event for the id means the approval is spent, whatever its own chunks say. If the send fails, nothing runs and the run stays parked until the next resume. **Rejected:** the merged log alone (a host that consumed, ran and crashed before pushing leaves no line any other host can see).
- **Q11. A scope that outlives what it was for** (R28 S-11, ruled). **Plan's reading:** a `main` session (a proxy's DM, which lives for years) offers only `once` at every tier; elsewhere T2 offers `once` and `session`, and a `session` allowance ends at the earlier of 24 h after its decision and the session's `close`, after which the same call parks again.
- **Q12. Who writes the summary** (R28 S-10, ruled). **Plan's reading:** `summary` is composed by keeper from a template per tool over the record's own arguments (`drive_write` → "Write `<path>` in <drive> (<n> bytes)"; `declassify` → "Let <readers> read <what> (<sha256 prefix>)"), never from model text; `ApprovalRecord::new` takes no summary argument, and a later tool adds its template with its tier row (epics 94–96).

## The failure shape this epic must not repeat

**An approval that authorises something else.** A review that finds any of the following is a blocker:
- a field that changes what runs but is not in `binding_digest`, or a record whose `id`, `session` or `agent` is not bound;
- a float in a digested field accepted, or two hosts computing different digests for one record;
- a `summary` that carries the model's words;
- an approval record edited after it was written, or a decision file written by anyone but the claim holder, or by a phone;
- an `always` scope, `session` from T3 up, `session` in a `main` session, or a `session` allowance that outlives 24 h.

**An approval used twice.** A review that finds an effect before the homeserver has accepted its `consumed` event and the host has read back that the event is the first for the id, a resume that reads its own log before the room, or an executor re-run after "effect unknown", is a blocker.

**A yes from the wrong place.** A review that finds a decision accepted from an unencrypted event, an unverified device, an agent user, a person outside the label, a person nobody pinned, or — at T4 — a person other than the requester or the owning host's own process, is a blocker; so is any code that writes `master_key`.

**A thread that waits.** A review that finds an agent turn blocking in `block_in_place` or polling for a decision is a blocker; parking returns.
